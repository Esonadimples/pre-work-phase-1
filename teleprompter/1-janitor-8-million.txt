He swept floors at JCPenney
and wore a thrift-store coat.

When he died,
he left $8 million
to a library and a hospital.


His name was Ronald Read,
and he ran a gas station for decades.

He drove an old Toyota
and held his coat together
with a safety pin.

Nobody in his town knew
he owned shares in 95 companies.


He never picked winners.

He just bought good companies
and never sold them.


He started buying stocks in the 1950s
with money from his paycheck.

He bought blue-chip names
like P&G and Johnson & Johnson.

He reinvested every dividend,
year after year,
for about 60 years.

By 2014,
those small buys
had grown into $8 million.


Wealth isn't about earning more.
It's about buying steadily
and not touching it.

Time did the work he couldn't.


I'm Dimples.
I talk about the things we feel
but never say out loud.
Follow for more.
